## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** The site wears a PowerShell terminal costume over a stock developer-portfolio skeleton. The metaphor is the only personality layer, and it is decorative rather than structural: `PS>` labels announce sections that are then ordinary card grids. Strip `.term-bar`, `.cmd::before`, and `.grad-text` and nothing identifies this as Benoit's site rather than any Azure consultant's. Category-interchangeable: the aurora radial-gradient background, the `.glass` card, the counting stats, the gradient headline. Missed character: no featured article or tool preview (the homepage of a tools hub previews zero tools), no outcome from any of the 22 CAC40 clients, no Azure-derived palette. The strongest genuinely-authored element is the ascending experience timeline.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs --json index.html` exited 2 with 4 findings, all verified, zero false positives: `gradient-text` (line 125), `layout-transition` (line 370, `transition: width` on `.track i`), `dark-glow` (line 319, `.tl-dot` zero-offset halo), `marquee` (`.news-banner-text` infinite loop). The detector caught the gradient-text pattern that turns out to be a live WCAG failure; it did not catch the broken dismiss button, the undefined token, the light-mode contrast failures, or the heading inversion — those came from browser measurement.

**Visual overlays.** Not injected. Evidence was gathered by direct `page.evaluate` measurement against a local static server, now stopped. No user-visible overlay exists.

## Overall Impression

The engineering discipline is real — zero dependencies, a complete two-theme token system, FOUC prevention, `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls`, alt text on all 50 images, two `prefers-reduced-motion` blocks, a rAF-throttled passive scroll listener. That is better hygiene than most hand-built sites.

What undercuts it is that the page is a résumé pretending to be a hub. It lists credentials exhaustively and shows work not at all, then offers no way to move through 4,766 px of it. The single biggest opportunity: cut the résumé to a third of its length and spend the reclaimed space previewing the blog posts and tools that the whole site exists to distribute.

## What's Working

1. **The experience timeline.** The ascending gradient rail (`.timeline::before`, green -> accent -> violet), the arrowhead terminus, the `▲ growth` note, and the per-role dots make a chronology read as a trajectory. That is authored design, not a template.
2. **The theme system.** `#0c1420` is a genuinely dark dark, not the lazy mid-grey most sites ship, and every semantic token has a real dark counterpart. The pre-paint inline script means no flash. Dark mode contrast measured 6.1:1 to 18.5:1 across every sampled pair — zero failures.
3. **Motion restraint.** Two separate reduced-motion blocks, and they cover the right things: the marquee is fully neutralised (`animation:none; padding-left:0`) and `.reveal` is forced visible rather than left broken. Most sites that ship a marquee never do this.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The "Dismiss" button on the news banner does not dismiss the banner
- **What**: `newsBannerClose` sets `banner.setAttribute("hidden","")` (line 865), but `.news-banner { display: flex }` (line 150) is an author rule that beats the UA `[hidden] { display: none }`. Measured after clicking: `hiddenAttr: true`, `computedDisplay: "flex"`, still 40 px tall.
- **Why it matters**: The one interactive control that promises to remove something removes nothing. The user clicks, nothing happens, they click again. It only disappears on the *next* page load via `data-news-banner`. This ships on all 63 pages of the site.
- **Fix**: Add `.news-banner[hidden] { display: none; }`, or have the handler set `document.documentElement.setAttribute("data-news-banner","hidden")` so it uses the path that already works.

### [P0] The GitHub call-to-action links to GitHub's homepage
- **What**: Line 756, `href="https://github.com/"`.
- **Why it matters**: It sits in the contact block beside Mail and LinkedIn, at the moment of highest intent. A visitor who wants to see the code lands on GitHub marketing. For someone whose credibility rests on IaC and automation, a dead portfolio link is the worst possible broken link.
- **Fix**: `https://github.com/Benoit-Gaumard`.

### [P1] The name in the `<h1>` fails contrast, in light mode, as the first thing anyone reads
- **What**: `.grad-text` paints text with `-webkit-text-fill-color: transparent` over `linear-gradient(100deg, var(--cp-cyan), var(--cp-accent) 45%, var(--cp-violet))`. Measured against `--cp-bg`: cyan stop **2.44:1**, on `--cp-surface` **2.57:1**. Large text needs 3:1. Also hits all three `.stat .n` numbers. Two more light-mode failures: `.skill h3` and `.exp-card .hash` at `#c88719` on white = **3.03:1** (need 4.5:1), and `.tl-note` at **4.46:1**.
- **Why it matters**: "Benoit Gaumard" is the single most important string on the page and its left third is unreadable for low-vision users. Dark mode is clean, so this is purely a light-palette defect.
- **Fix**: Start the gradient at `--cp-accent` instead of `--cp-cyan` in light mode, or darken `--cp-cyan` to ≈`#0a7f9b`. Darken `--cp-warning` to ≈`#8a5b00` for light. Darken `--cp-success` slightly for `.tl-note`.

### [P1] Six section anchors exist; nothing links to any of them
- **What**: `#about`, `#experience`, `#certifications`, `#skills`, `#tools`, `#contact` are all present and all unreferenced (measured). The header carries four site-level links only.
- **Why it matters**: 4,766 px of linear scroll. A recruiter who wants certifications must scroll the entire experience timeline and its 22 client chips. The IDs prove the wayfinding was intended and then dropped.
- **Fix**: A sticky secondary nav row under the header, or a floating mini-TOC. The markup is already there.

### [P1] Heading structure is broken, and `<section>` landmarks are unnamed
- **What**: Measured outline is `h1` -> fifteen `h3` -> one `h2` last. Section titles are `<p class="sec-label">`, not headings. All six `<section>` elements have no `aria-label`/`aria-labelledby`.
- **Why it matters**: Screen-reader users navigate long pages by heading and by landmark. Here both are unusable: the heading list is a flat wall of fifteen `h3`s with no section boundaries, and the landmark list is six anonymous "region"s. This is the accessibility cost of the `sec-label` styling choice.
- **Fix**: Make each `.sec-label` an `<h2>` (keep the visual styling), demote the contact `h2`, and add `aria-labelledby` on each `<section>` pointing at its new `h2`.

### [P2] No `<noscript>` and no print stylesheet: two ways to get a blank page
- **What**: `.reveal { opacity: 0 }` by default; only `IntersectionObserver` clears it. Measured on load: 1 of 6 sections revealed. There is no `<noscript>` (0 found) and no `@media print` (none found).
- **Why it matters**: JS failure, an aggressive content blocker, or Ctrl+P on a résumé site all yield a hero and then nothing. People print and PDF résumés.
- **Fix**: Add `<noscript><style>.reveal{opacity:1;transform:none}</style></noscript>` and an `@media print` block that reveals everything, drops the aurora, and hides the banner/back-to-top.
